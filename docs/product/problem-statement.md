# Problem Statement

Last updated: 2026-09-23

## Purpose

Define the real problem this project exists to solve.

Agents should read this before proposing features, architecture, or UI. A
project that cannot name its problem clearly will drift into building surfaces
that look complete but do not create value.

## Problem

- Primary problem: company state, decisions, responsibilities, evidence, and
  supervised automation drift across tools and become difficult to understand
  as one accountable operating system.
- Who has this problem: initially small-company owners coordinating people,
  providers, and supervised agents; the product should remain configurable for
  other companies and industries.
- Current workaround: the owner reconstructs company state from separate tools,
  conversations, documents, integrations, and manual follow-up.
- Why the workaround is painful: orientation takes too long, assumptions and
  decisions can be lost, decisions are delayed, responsibility becomes unclear,
  and completed work may lack inspectable evidence. Application assumptions can
  also be mixed with descriptive documentation, leaving the owner unable to tell
  which assumptions are not implemented, implemented correctly, implemented
  incorrectly, or still unverified.
- What changes if this problem is solved: the owner can move through one daily
  loop from attention, to decision, to accountable delegation, to evidence of
  completion, while agents perform more work without constant supervision. For
  the reference MVP, the owner and an authorized Roost agent must use that loop
  to finish one already-started application against accepted assumptions, prove
  its readiness for sale as a subscription product, and leave a process reusable
  for subsequent applications. Digital-service sales follow later.

## Constraints

- Business constraints: validate priorities using a real reference installation
  while keeping the product installable and configurable for other companies.
- Technical constraints: preserve workspace scoping, a canonical operational
  record, API-governed access, and separation between reusable behavior and
  private installation configuration.
- Legal/security constraints: do not expose private company data, credentials,
  or installation details as public product defaults or examples.
- Time or resource constraints: establish the dependable web, backend, and MCP
  foundation before adding a native mobile channel or selecting a broader
  commercial delivery model.

## Non-Solutions

List approaches that appear tempting but do not solve the real problem.

| Non-solution | Why it is insufficient |
| --- | --- |
| A one-off application hard-coded for the reference company | It cannot be safely configured or reused by another company and leaks installation assumptions into the product. |
| A generic dashboard without a connected operating loop | It may display information but does not help the owner decide, delegate, and verify outcomes. |
| Automation without accountable ownership and evidence | It reduces visible effort while increasing uncertainty and supervision risk. |
| Using an AI agent for every repeatable integration step | It spends model capacity on deterministic work, increases variability, and obscures where a callback or API should provide a testable result. |
| Continuing paid access or delivery to avoid confronting non-payment | It converts a clear commercial exchange into unbounded credit, creates avoidable disputes, and spends company capacity without confirmed revenue. |
| Treating acceptance, defects, extra requests, and final handover as one informal conversation | It makes completion unverifiable, encourages scope disputes, and obscures what the customer purchased versus what the company may reuse. |
| Leaving review, warranty, and maintenance without time boundaries | It lets completed projects remain indefinitely open and makes ordinary support indistinguishable from included defect correction. |
| Accepting material instructions from anyone in any channel | It creates contradictory commitments and makes authority impossible to prove. |
| Continuing a harmful or commercially impossible engagement at any cost | It turns sunk effort or expected revenue into pressure to ignore safety, competence, ethics, or reliable delivery. |
| Shared credentials, permanent broad access, or production-first work on customer data | It weakens attribution, makes revocation unreliable, and exposes more customer data and systems than the accepted result requires. |
| Making future customer-environment infrastructure a blocker for the application-first MVP | It spends limited capacity on a later service-delivery problem before the current owner-and-agent product-completion proof exists. |
| Calling an application ready because many secondary features work while its primary user cannot repeatedly complete the core outcome | It measures feature volume instead of the promised customer value. |
| Waiving a critical readiness blocker or keeping an undocumented limitation to release sooner | It converts known product, security, recovery, payment, or operating risk into an undisclosed customer problem. |

## Evidence

- User evidence: the owner accepted a configurable multi-installation direction,
  the daily attention-to-evidence loop, and the full outcome set below on
  2026-09-20.
- Market/domain evidence: not yet collected for this interview baseline.
- Internal evidence: the current product and architecture already define a
  self-hosted, workspace-scoped operating system with human and agent access.
- Open assumptions: the versioning and migration contract for custom
  departments; when a capability genuinely needs separate packaging instead of
  contextual reuse; the private application-specific identity, problem, core
  path, acceptance details, and severity thresholds inside the accepted general
  product-ready and sale-ready rules; the status and suitability of any existing
  application-assessment mechanism; the exact first-sale financial capability
  boundary after the accepted deterministic-first and sandbox-to-live proof;
  provider, invoice, and document authority; the offering-level size boundary,
  deposit percentage, milestone amounts, pricing, per-offer review windows, and exact
  jurisdiction-appropriate rights/licence language; the later service-sales and
  service-delivery contracts; exact retention periods for customer-project
  working copies and evidence; the design and funding trigger for later isolated
  customer environments and anonymization; the baseline and numeric targets for
  each accepted success outcome.
