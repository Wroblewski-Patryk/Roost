# Non-Goals

Last updated: 2026-09-24

## Purpose

List what the project is intentionally not doing now.

Non-goals protect focus. They also stop agents from reviving old ideas as if
they were active scope.

## Current Non-Goals

| ID | Non-goal | Reason | Revisit trigger | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| NG-002 | Build native mobile before the web owner loop works reliably. | Splitting effort across channels would weaken validation of the primary control plane. | The web loop is usable and its success outcomes can be measured honestly. | Product owner |
| NG-003 | Add native billing, a marketplace, or hosted multi-tenant SaaS in the current phase. | The accepted model is independently installed self-hosted Roost; its value must be proven before selecting a broader commercial delivery model. | Repeatable self-hosted adoption plus an explicit monetization and hosting decision. | Product owner |
| NG-004 | Build a full native CRM suite by default. | Shared customer capabilities and approved providers should satisfy demonstrated workflows before Roost duplicates a complete specialist product. | Evidence that important accepted workflows cannot be served safely by shared capabilities or integrations. | Product owner |
| NG-005 | Require invitation delivery and multi-human onboarding for current product validation. | The current operating cohort is one human owner plus supervised agents; existing invitation capability does not have to define the present success gate. | The web foundation is working and multi-human operation becomes an actual owner need. | Product owner |
| NG-006 | Deliver a complete owner data export in the current MVP. | The direction is accepted, but the MVP must first prove the core service and subscription operating flows. | The core business flows work and a later data-lifecycle phase is explicitly opened. | Product owner |
| NG-007 | Deliver company archive and permanent deletion in the current MVP. | Safe deletion depends on export, retention, backup, audit, and provider-boundary decisions that belong to a later phase. | Export and retention contracts are accepted and lifecycle administration becomes an active need. | Product owner |
| NG-008 | Deliver the complete provider disconnect and optional purge lifecycle in the current MVP. | The direction is accepted, but core integration use and synchronization must be validated first. | A stable provider integration exists and disconnection becomes a demonstrated operating need. | Product owner |
| NG-009 | Build prospect collection or the full digital-service sales and delivery flow before the application-completion MVP is proven. | The owner selected product completion as Roost's first business proof. Parallel service expansion would dilute that validation. | One existing application reaches accepted sale readiness and the completion process is reusable. | Product owner |
| NG-010 | Select or implement payment, invoice, or accounting providers before the open sale-readiness boundary is decided. | Automated financial operations are the expected direction, but the provider, source of truth, document flow, accounting duties, failure recovery, and agent authority are not yet defined. | The owner accepts the first-sale boundary and the governed financial-operation contract. | Product owner |
| NG-011 | Create a parallel assumption audit or application-readiness system before evaluating what already exists. | The owner reports that a similar process may have been started, but its completion and canonical authority are unknown. A second mechanism would increase the exact documentation drift Roost must solve. | Existing product records and documentation have been inventoried, classified, and found insufficient through an explicit product decision. | Product owner |
| NG-012 | Duplicate every application's user-account, entitlement, and access-control panel inside Roost. | Each application owns its operational user and subscription-access state. Roost needs the company-level customer, offering, revenue, invoice, accounting, and exception context instead. | A demonstrated cross-application company workflow cannot be served by references, summaries, or governed integration events. | Product owner |
| NG-013 | Use an AI agent or routine manual browser work for a stable, repeatable financial step that a tested callback, API, or script can perform safely. | Deterministic automation is cheaper, easier to test and reconcile, and leaves agent capacity for judgment, exceptions, and application work. | The provider lacks a safe integration path, the case is genuinely exceptional, or human/agent judgment is required. | Product owner |
| NG-014 | Turn every customer comment or feature suggestion directly into implementation work. | Support questions, confirmed defects, and feature proposals require different handling. Automatic task creation would create backlog noise and imply commitments without a product decision. | The item is classified as a confirmed defect or an authorized product decision accepts work on the proposal. | Product owner |
| NG-015 | Continue paid product access or chargeable service delivery without confirmed payment merely to retain or persuade a customer. | The accepted commercial exchange is explicit: unpaid customers do not receive paid value, and accepted scope alone does not authorize work on credit. | A deliberate free offer, bounded pre-sale activity, contractual grace period, or separately authorized exception is explicitly defined. | Product owner |
| NG-016 | Treat detailed discovery output, an informal extra request, or work outside accepted scope as a free sales activity. | The free stage qualifies the opportunity; reusable analysis and additional delivery consume company capacity and require an accepted commercial condition. | The company deliberately defines a bounded promotional deliverable or separately authorizes the exception with visible cost. | Product owner |
| NG-017 | Classify a new function, changed expectation, or changed direction as a free defect correction. | A defect is a failure against accepted scope or acceptance criteria; new value belongs to a priced scope change. | Evidence shows that the requested result was already part of the accepted scope or criteria. | Product owner |
| NG-018 | Transfer reusable company tools, libraries, methods, or components automatically with every customer deliverable. | Project-specific results and agreed rights can be transferred after settlement without surrendering reusable company capability that supports other work. | A specific agreement explicitly prices and transfers or licenses the asset differently. | Product owner |
| NG-019 | Provide indefinite free defect correction, maintenance, updates, or ordinary support after a service project closes. | A defined warranty covers failures against accepted criteria; ongoing operation is a distinct service and cost. | The offering explicitly includes a longer support period or a separately paid maintenance arrangement is active. | Product owner |
| NG-020 | Ignore a critical security event because the warranty or maintenance period ended. | Critical risk requires immediate assessment to protect affected parties and determine responsibility, even when the eventual remedy is not free. | Never; only the responsibility, remedy, and commercial allocation are decided after assessment. | Product owner |
| NG-021 | Treat every customer participant, meeting comment, email, or chat message as authority to change scope, price, schedule, or acceptance. | One explicit customer decision authority prevents contradictory instructions and accidental commitments. | The engagement records an authorized replacement or bounded delegation. | Product owner |
| NG-022 | Continue an illegal, unethical, unsafe, materially insecure, abusive, or commercially untenable engagement merely because work or payment has begun. | Revenue does not override legal, safety, competence, security, or reliable-delivery boundaries. | The blocking condition is resolved and a recorded authorized decision confirms safe continuation. | Product owner |
| NG-023 | Use shared customer credentials or grant broad permanent customer-system access to a person or agent. | Individual attribution, least privilege, and bounded duration are required for accountability and safe revocation. | Never as a default; a system limitation may require an explicitly accepted, temporary, audited exception. | Product owner |
| NG-024 | Make dedicated customer test/staging environments or anonymization infrastructure a requirement of the current application-first MVP. | Customer-service delivery follows later, and the owner first needs supervised agents to work under the already accepted local runtime baseline. | The service-delivery phase opens and revenue or other resources can support the required infrastructure. | Product owner |
| NG-025 | Leave company or agent access and task-owned client working copies active indefinitely after project closure. | Unnecessary access and copies increase security, privacy, and accountability risk after their purpose ends. | Only an explicit legal, accounting, warranty, security, deliverable, or agreed business-evidence basis authorizes bounded retention. | Product owner |
| NG-026 | Declare an application product-ready or sale-ready while a known critical blocker remains in a required gate. | A label cannot replace the core user outcome or the security, recovery, commercial, and operating conditions needed to deliver it safely. | The blocker is resolved and verified, or new evidence proves it was classified incorrectly. | Product owner |
| NG-027 | Hide a known limitation or accept it indefinitely without impact, workaround, accountability, and review. | Undocumented exceptions become accidental product promises and unresolved risk, while demanding absolute perfection would prevent useful release. | A non-critical limitation is explicitly accepted under the governed readiness-exception rule. | Product owner |

## Rule

If work touches a non-goal, stop and create an open decision before
implementation.
